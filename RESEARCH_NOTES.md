# HUNTER Aesthetic Build — Research Notes

## Programming principles used
- Train each major muscle about twice weekly through the A/B split.
- Use mostly 2–4 hard sets per exercise and systematic progressive overload.
- Most working sets use moderate rep ranges; isolation work uses somewhat higher reps.
- Work close to failure, but do not require momentary failure on every set.
- Prefer controlled, comfortable full ROM; use lengthened-position-biased choices where evidence is useful.
- Keep exercise variation systematic rather than random.

## Aesthetic priorities
The app emphasizes upper chest, lateral delts, lats/upper back, arms, quads/hamstrings, calves and abs. This is a physique-design priority rather than a claim that one muscle is scientifically the single biggest determinant of attractiveness.

## Key exercise decisions
- ~30° incline pressing: useful for upper-pec emphasis without turning the movement into a shoulder-dominant press.
- Lateral raises: direct lateral-delt work; cable and dumbbell versions both have evidence for similar hypertrophy.
- Overhead triceps extension: useful long-head triceps emphasis.
- Seated leg curl: long-length hamstring training has produced greater hypertrophy than prone curls in a controlled study.
- 45° back extension: used as the posterior-chain replacement for Romanian deadlifts, with a simpler setup and a strong glute/hamstring contribution.
- Standing calf raise: strong practical choice for gastrocnemius development; seated work can be added later if desired.
- Pulling variations are changed systematically across A/B days, but the app does not claim that a wider grip automatically creates "lat width" while a close grip creates "thickness" because evidence does not justify that simple rule.
